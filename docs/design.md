# Design

The site is a quiet sheet laid over a dithered bamboo grove that bends in the wind. The layout still snaps to an invisible grid.

## The sheet

- `--cell` (96px desktop, 48px under 768px) is the grid pitch; `--row` is 96px everywhere.
- The grid is not drawn. It only sets sizes.
- The frame width snaps to whole pairs of cells (`round(down, 100%, cell*2)`), so its edges sit on cell boundaries. Rows, tiles and columns are whole numbers of cells. When adding a block, size it in `--cell` / `--row`, never in free pixels.

## Layers (bottom to top)

| z           | What                                                                                      |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--z-field` | the backdrop: the dithered bamboo (`BambooField.astro`), or the shelved koi pond or fire  |
| `--z-page`  | rail and page content                                                                     |
| `--z-noise` | the pulsing grain over everything (`body::after`, moved by transform, pointer-events off) |

## Pieces

- **Rail** (`Header.astro`): the signature, the nav, and the theme and language switches. Nav links answer hover with an underline that fills from the left like a progress bar and drains out to the right on leaving; the current page has no underline, only the accent colour. At its foot sit the two switches, then, under a short hairline, the links and the copyright, every line on one rhythm. It is sticky on desktop and a two-row block on phones. There is no visible footer; `Footer.astro` is the colophon inside the rail.
- **Signature** (`Signature.astro`): the Owner's mark, the home link at the top of the rail: one angular silhouette (a top bar and a bottom bar joined by a diagonal) traced from the Owner's logo, one grid cell wide (two on phones). It is filled with a vertical gradient inside an edge with its own gradient, all from `--color-mark-*`: deep indigo to violet in light mode; in dark, white at the top through pale lime to a solid lime at the foot, inside a grey edge that turns olive (the colours of the Owner's green logo). The blue is the dark site accent only, not the mark. On the first page of a visit the edge draws itself round the shape, then the fill comes up; the `signed` class (from sessionStorage) shows it whole on later pages.
- **Page heads**: a page's title block is one row (96px) tall at least, so the content starts close under the title.
- **Theme**: `data-theme` on `<html>`, set before first paint. Light mode is magma (cream paper, pink, magenta, plum, deep indigo ink); dark mode is green-black paper with a blue accent. Every colour is a token in `src/styles/tokens.css`. Switching crossfades through a View Transition.
- **Backdrop**: one animated layer behind every page, picked by `site.backdrop` in `src/lib/site.ts`. Now `bamboo`; `koi` and `flame` are set aside but still work.
- **Bamboo** (`BambooField.astro`, `src/scripts/bamboo-field.ts`): a grove grown fresh on every load from a random seed: three to seven culms in all, in two or three clumps along the foot of the viewport (one or two on phones), spaced apart and leaning a little toward the middle. Within a clump the culms stand well apart and fan outward. Every culm has its own height, girth (from a whip to a thick pole) and taper; side branches grow from most of the upper nodes, and about half the culms end in a crown of short leafy twigs while the rest end bare. Painted as ink and dithered with an 8x8 Bayer matrix at one dot per 2 css px. Physics: each culm is a chain of angular springs, stiff at the base and loose at the tip; branches grow from the upper nodes as shorter chains, and sprays of 3 to 7 leaves fan from them, each leaf its own spring. A slow breeze sways it all; a moving pointer adds only a faint gust (sampled every 50ms, capped low, falling off over 120px, fading over 1.4s) that stirs the nearby leaves and hardly moves the culms. The pose is rebuilt from the roots each frame. Painting runs on the GPU (WebGL1), so the CPU only steps the springs and lays out vertices: every culm segment, node ring, branch and leaf becomes translucent triangles drawn into a framebuffer at twice the dither resolution, so overlapping leaves build density as in ink wash; a full-screen shader then dithers the density into three steps and the warmth (cool teal-green or warm yellow-green, per spray) into the two families. Back culms are fainter. Inks come from `--color-bamboo-cool-1..3` and `--color-bamboo-warm-1..3` (faint to strong), strength from `--bamboo-strength`. Capped at 30fps, paused in hidden tabs, one still frame under reduced motion. Without WebGL or script the paper simply shows.
- **Koi pond, set aside** (`KoiPond.astro`, `src/scripts/koi-pond.ts`): a pond drawn in ASCII on a 2D canvas, a character grid of 7 x 12 css px cells (6 x 10 on phones). Each frame fills a cell buffer layer by layer and stamps it from a glyph atlas (one strip per ink and opacity step). Bottom to top: a tiled floor of pebble edges and boulders at the viewport's edges (lit from the top left, mossy on top, grass on their rims); the moving caustic web; water weed swaying up from the bottom and the stones; koi with their shadows on the floor; ripples where a fish touches the surface; lily pads (some in bloom) and drifting petals; overhanging foliage, some flowering, at the corners. Each koi is a chain of joints following a wandering head that steers clear of the edges, the stones and the other fish, so the body bends as it turns; it has a wagging forked tail, pectoral fins, eyes, an outline, and one of six varieties (kohaku, sanke, showa, tancho, ogon, asagi) in its own size. Inks come from `--color-pond-*` and `--color-koi-*`, strength from `--pond-strength`. Capped at 24fps, paused in hidden tabs, one still frame under reduced motion. Without script the paper simply shows.
- **Frosted text**: every block of text (page heads, section heads, post rows, prose, captions, the rail) sits on a soft frosted patch, feathered at its edges, so the backdrop stays in view around the words but never runs through a line. Over the bamboo the patches are drawn in the bamboo's own shader: the page's text blocks (listed in `src/scripts/frost.ts`) are measured as the page scrolls, and under each the ink is blurred over about 6px and thinned before it is dithered. The bamboo then sets `frost-in-field` on `<html>`, which stands down the CSS patches. Those (a `::before` with `backdrop-filter: blur(var(--legible-blur))` over a `--legible-wash`, feathered with a mask) remain for the shelved backdrops; a `backdrop-filter` is re-blurred on every composited frame, which over an animated backdrop and the pulsing grain was the page's largest CPU cost.
- **Fire, set aside** (`FlameField.astro`, `src/scripts/flame-field.ts`): one WebGL fragment shader at a third of the viewport resolution, stretched with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Turbulence scrolls upward and is cut off at a height that wanders across the width and over time, so the flame is solid at its base and splits into pointed tongues near the tip. Two layers of sparks rise out of it, sway as they climb, cool as they go and die out at their own heights. Each spark is a glow about one field pixel wide, fed into the same dither and colour ramp as the flame, so it shows as a single dot of the flame's own colour. Everything is quantised with an 8x8 Bayer dither, and nothing is drawn below a floor, so there is no loose speckle. Light mode burns magenta to pink to peach; dark mode burns emerald. Colours come from `--color-ember-hot`, `--color-ember` and `--color-ember-cool`, and strength from `--field-strength`, re-read on theme change. It is capped at 30fps, paused in hidden tabs, and drawn as one still frame under reduced motion. Without WebGL or JS the paper simply shows.
- **Photo wall** (`PhotoWall.astro`, `src/scripts/photo-wall.ts`, `src/scripts/dither.ts`): masonry, each card at its photo's own ratio. The order is the Owner's (Studio → Archive → Photo order): with script the wall is a grid of 4px rows and each card spans the rows it needs, so photos read left to right, then down; without script it falls back to CSS `columns`, which fill each column top to bottom. At rest a card shows its photo in Unicode braille (U+2800 to U+283F, 2x3 dots per character). The photo is sampled at dot resolution and Atkinson-dithered: dots carry the darks on light paper and the lights on dark paper. Each character takes a tone from `--color-dither-1` (shadow) to `--color-dither-5` (highlight) by the brightness under it, and the tones shift hue as they climb (magma: indigo, plum, raspberry, peach; dark: navy, blue, azure, ice, matching the blue accent), so the picture reads as a gradient. The 64 glyphs are drawn from the font once per tone into an atlas and stamped onto a whole-device-pixel grid, about 4 css px per character, with the dots thickened a little by a stroke. If no braille font is present the plain photo shows. The braille is drawn when the card's lazy image loads and redrawn on resize and theme change. How a card answers a hovering fine pointer is set by `site.cardHover` in `src/lib/site.ts` (as `data-hover` on the wall). In both, the braille gives way to the photo (and on keyboard focus). Now `pop`: the card eases up to 1.03 on a spring damped just past critical, so it settles without a bounce, with a faint shadow. Set aside but still working, `foil`: the card lifts and tilts toward the cursor under two radial layers centred on the cursor: a glare, bright under the pointer and falling off to shade at the far edge (overlay, `--color-glare`, `-mid`, `-shade`), and a faint holographic sheen in rings of the theme's own tints (`--color-foil-1` to `-4`, at `--foil-strength`). Touch keeps the braille. Each card shows the photo's pixel size (as `1600×1067`) in muted 12px text on a small square plate of paper at its top right; it fades out while the photo shows through (hover or keyboard focus). Without JS the plain photo shows.
- **Code**: fences in posts are highlighted at build time by Shiki (`markdoc.config.mjs`, no client JS) with its css-variables theme, so every syntax colour is a `--shiki-*` token in `tokens.css`: magma inks on cream in light mode, blue, lime and sea inks in dark. The language comes from the fence (` ```ts `); a fence without one stays plain.
- **Works** (`WorkCard.astro`): square tiles of whole cells. A Work with a cover shows it above its words; one without becomes a text tile (status, title, summary, then its stack).
- **Links** (`LinkList.astro`): kept apart from the photos as text rows, site then title and note.
- **Lightbox** (`src/scripts/lightbox.ts`): a `<dialog>` slideshow. Opening morphs the card into the slide: same box, same live tilt, its dither dissolving as the plane flattens. Previous / Next sit under the photo; they, ArrowLeft / ArrowRight and a swipe on touch all change photo with a short fade (out in 110ms, in through a light blur in 260ms), and pressing again mid-fade cuts straight on. A click anywhere but the photo and controls, Escape, or Close morphs back into the current card. Without JS each card links to the large rendition.
- **Back arrow** (`PostView.astro`): an arrow-only link above each post's title, back to the Writing index (labelled for screen readers). Hover or focus slides the arrow left and brightens it to the accent; pressing pulls it further and scales it to 0.9. Transform only, so reduced motion just cuts it.
- **Press feedback**: every pressable scales down on `:active` (0.97 for text controls, 0.99 for rows and cards, 160ms, strong ease-out); links inside prose dim instead. Hover-only effects are gated on `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.

## Rules

- Tokens only: no literal colours or font families outside `tokens.css`.
- Animate `transform`, `opacity` and `filter` only; every animation has a reduced-motion fallback.
- Easing: `--ease-out` (cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)) for anything answering the user, `--ease-in-out` for movement on screen, never ease-in.
- Body text is 13px Helvetica Neue by design. Raise `--text-prose` if long posts need it.
- No tracked capitals, no middle-dot meta strings, no arrows glued to links.
